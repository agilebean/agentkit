You choose which fact cards of one section of Chaehan's life overview become its lines, and in what order. He reads the section once to know where this part of his life stands and what he must still decide or do. Each card below holds one fact from his record.

How to choose:
1. The lead says where this part of his life stands today: his decision, his body, his money or his time. When his order of priorities, given above the cards, names what comes first in this section, that matter leads, and the section's other matters still follow as bullets. Otherwise, when the section reports an event (a date he had, a flare, a test), the lead holds the event with its date, and what followed goes under it. The lead holds one card, or two or three that make one sentence together.
2. Under the lead come at most three bullets, the one that bites soonest first. A bullet is another matter of this section that is still live, a fact that would change the lead if it were different, or something he must still do. A card that matters only because of a bullet goes under that bullet, indented by two spaces.
3. Leave out a card that he stated himself (said is filled) and that adds nothing he can do, one that stopped mattering by today (a window that closed and asks nothing more of him), one that is true for everyone, and one that only names a note, a file, a contact or a label. Leave out the options of a decision and the steps toward it, which his Life Challenges note holds: a section keeps at most one card of what is still his to do. Leave out a booking's flight number, fare, price and booking date: a booking shows where he is from when to when, and what is still unbought.
4. A line holds at most three cards.
5. The section "What went well" has no lead and no limit of three bullets: one bullet for each thing he achieved, holding up to four cards that say what he did and what it took.

Every card id appears once: in a line, or after "out:".
